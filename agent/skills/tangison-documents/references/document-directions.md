## Page-role planning

For every multi-page document, write a table before building:

| Page | Job | Dominant content | Architecture | Logo treatment | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Establish | Title, recipient, promise | Selected cover/opening family | Primary mark | Generic cover |
| 2 | Orient | Context, executive summary | Bright editorial split | Small folio + edge watermark | Text density |
| 3 | Prove | Scope, evidence, figures | Ledger or dominant visual | Symbol crop | Table overflow |

Continue until every page has a distinct job. Do not leave “continue content” as the only job unless it is a legal continuation page.

## Composition families

- **Asymmetric masthead:** logo and metadata occupy opposite visual weights; ideal for quotations and letters.
- **Quiet ledger:** strong numeric hierarchy, fine rules, generous white space; ideal for invoices and statements.
- **Editorial split:** unequal columns, one dominant message, supporting details; ideal for proposals and profiles.
- **Evidence board:** annotated screenshots or images with concise findings; ideal for audits and reports.
- **Chapter field:** mostly open space, oversized title, cropped watermark or motif; ideal for long reports.
- **Ceremonial axis:** precise symmetry with controlled scale and borders; ideal for certificates and formal recognitions.
- **Legal rhythm:** wide margins, restrained headings, clause numbering, generous leading; ideal for agreements.
- **Contact close:** bold contact hierarchy and minimal final statement; ideal for back covers.

## Variation rules

- One- to two-page documents may use one composition family with a clear hierarchy shift.
- Three- to five-page documents must use at least two families.
- Six pages or more must use at least three families.
- A repeated table format does not count as a new family.
- A colour inversion alone does not count as a new family.
- Preserve one grid, type system, folio logic, and motif so variation does not become inconsistency.

## Logo cadence patterns

### Compact commercial

Use for quotes, invoices, receipts, and statements of work.

1. Primary logo in opening masthead.
2. Small mark in footer or folio on continuation pages.
3. Optional single watermark on a terms or payment page.

### Editorial branded

Use for proposals, profiles, audits, and reports.

1. Primary logo on cover.
2. Small brand signature on every internal page.
3. Watermark on alternating or chapter-opening pages.
4. One oversized cropped symbol on a separator or closing page.

### Formal restrained

Use for contracts, NDAs, and letters.

1. Primary logo on title or first page.
2. Small monochrome mark in folio/header on continuation pages.
3. Watermark only when it does not reduce legal readability.

### Ceremonial

Use for certificates.

1. Primary mark in a clear prestige zone.
2. Large pale emblem or symbol in the field.
3. Signature or seal marks at the bottom; never crowd the recipient name.
